Understand

What's happening in your body, explained simply

🔄
Why the scales stopped moving
Metabolic adaptation explained

Your body is extraordinarily clever. When you eat 1,200 calories for a long time, it gradually adjusts to function on that amount — slowing digestion, reducing body temperature regulation, and burning fewer calories at rest — until the deficit you created essentially disappears.

This isn't failure. This is your body doing exactly what it evolved to do: survive. But it means the approach that worked at 50 no longer works at 66, especially after the activity drop following your ankle fracture.

This is why eating slightly more — not less — is the first step. Raising your calories to 1,400–1,500 with protein as the priority signals to your body that it's safe, and begins lifting your metabolic rate again.

The short version: Eating too little for too long trains your body to need less. The fix is to eat slightly more of the right things — especially protein — while adding strength exercise.
💧
Why the scales swing so wildly
Water weight and fibromyalgia

Normal daily weight fluctuation for anyone is 1–5 lbs — caused by food and water in your system, sweat, hydration levels, and the time of day you weigh yourself.

For you, this is amplified by fibromyalgia. Chronic pain keeps a hormone called cortisol elevated in your blood. Cortisol tells your kidneys to hold on to water and sodium — meaning you can retain noticeably more fluid than someone without chronic pain.

You could eat perfectly, sleep well, exercise well, and still see the scale go up 4–7 lbs overnight and back down the next day. This is not fat gain. It is your body's stress response doing its job.

The short version: Daily scale swings are mostly water — and fibromyalgia makes yours bigger than average. They tell you almost nothing about fat or muscle. Monthly measurements are enough.
💪
Why building muscle is the key
Sarcopenia and your metabolism

After 60, the body naturally loses muscle faster than it builds it — this is called sarcopenia. Muscle is "expensive" to maintain: every pound burns roughly 3× more calories at rest than fat does. As muscle is lost, your resting metabolism drops further.

The most effective way to reverse this is resistance exercise. Your body responds by rebuilding muscle fibres, which raises your metabolism, improves how your body handles blood sugar, and — critically for you — provides stronger support for your joints, reducing pain over time.

Important: When you start resistance training, the scale may go up slightly in the first 2–3 weeks. Muscle stores glycogen (energy) and water. This is a good sign. Keep going.

The short version: More muscle = higher metabolism at rest + better joint support + less pain. The early scale increase is muscle and water, not fat.
🍳
Why protein matters so much now
Fuelling muscle at 66

Protein is the raw material your body uses to build and repair muscle. Without enough of it, your body breaks down existing muscle tissue for energy — making sarcopenia worse.

90–100g of protein per day sounds like a lot, but it's achievable across three meals. It's also another reason 1,200 calories isn't enough — hitting 90g of protein on 1,200 calories while eating anything else is nearly impossible.

  • 2 eggs + 100g Greek yoghurt at breakfast = ~25g
  • 150g chicken or tinned fish at lunch = ~35g
  • 100g cottage cheese + protein at dinner = ~30g
Easy protein sources for your situation: Greek yoghurt, eggs, cottage cheese, tinned salmon or tuna, chicken breast, lentils, edamame — soft and easy to prepare, no hard gripping required.
🦴
Exercising with osteoarthritis
What helps and what to avoid

Osteoarthritis is where the cartilage cushioning your joints wears down. You have it in your fingers, wrists, and ankles. The common fear is that exercise makes it worse — but the evidence consistently shows the opposite. Appropriate exercise reduces OA pain over time by strengthening the muscles that support affected joints.

The key is the type of exercise:

  • Avoid: High-impact exercise, heavy barbells gripped with both hands, exercises that load the ankle in a restricted position
  • Good: Machine-based exercises, leg press instead of squats, chest press instead of push-ups, seated cable rows, swimming
  • For wrists: Use wrist wraps or padding where needed. Machines are far gentler than free weights
The short version: Movement is medicine for OA. The right exercise reduces pain — but it needs to be low-impact and machine-based for your joints.
🌊
Exercising with fibromyalgia
Pacing and the 24-hour rule

Fibromyalgia can cause a "flare" in pain or fatigue in the 24–48 hours after exercise — called post-exertion malaise. This is not a reason to avoid exercise, but it is a reason to start gently and increase slowly.

The key principle is pacing: start at an intensity that feels almost too easy. If you feel fine the next morning, you got the level right. If you're significantly worse, the session was too intense — not because you're weak, but because your nervous system processes effort differently with fibromyalgia.

The 24-hour check: After every gym session, note how you feel the next morning. Fine = right level. Significantly worse = scale back slightly. Never push through a fibromyalgia flare.
🏊
Why water exercise beats walking
The best cardio for your body

Walking is good — but for your specific situation, it isn't the best primary cardio. Your ankle is still problematic, you have OA in the ankle joint, and the toe numbness means prolonged walking can become uncomfortable quickly.

Pool walking and aqua aerobics give the same cardiovascular benefits with almost zero joint impact. Water provides about 12× more resistance than air, so you're working harder with far less stress on your feet and ankles. The warm water also actively reduces fibromyalgia pain signals while you're in it.

  • Best: Pool walking, aqua aerobics, gentle swimming
  • Good: Seated pedal exerciser at home, stationary bike at gym
  • Use cautiously: Walking — short distances (15–20 min) on flat ground are fine
  • Avoid: Running, aerobics classes, anything with jumping
The short version: Water is your best friend for cardio. If you can get to a pool even once a week, it's worth it.
🪞
Body dysmorphia and this process
Being kind to yourself as you change

Losing 100 lbs in your 50s was a remarkable achievement. But it also means you built your sense of yourself around a number on a scale — and when that number moved, it felt like you changed as a person.

The weight that has come back is not proof of failure. In large part, it is your body adapting to reduced activity after the ankle injury, combined with the natural muscle loss that comes with age. It happened to you — it is not something you did.

This programme is not about getting back to a number. It is about building a body that works well for you at 66 — that moves with less pain, has energy, and is strong enough for the things you want to do.

A reframe worth trying: Instead of "did I lose weight this week?" — ask "can I do more than I could last week?" That question always has an answer you can be proud of.
